Dual-sourced here means two independent counts of the same quantity, not two copies of the same count that happen to sit in different places.
Because the rest of the record reproduces from this instrument, these two numbers are the natural starting point for any recheck.
This is the most important fact in this section: the artefacts that would let a reproducer reconcile the two counts, byte by byte, no longer exist.
With those outputs gone, any attempt to close the gap would be guesswork, and the record does not guess.
The paper handles the disagreement by declining to pick a winner.
A reader who opens Table 1 expecting one produced-byte count for the first wave and finds two is looking at the intended presentation, not at a typo.
A reproducer who runs this computation should expect the same undefined result and should not read it as a mistake in their own working.
The contested numbers are load-bearing for the record's honesty, not for its results.
The bulk of the quantitative record can therefore be verified without touching the disputed wave at all.
